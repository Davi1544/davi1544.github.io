# Week 9 - The Importance of Preprocessing 

## Overview
I spent Week 9 adding two new preprocessing steps to our processing pipeline: dark current correction and reflectance calibration. In this post, we'll examine how these additions affect our dataset.

## Experiments

### Preprocessing tools: Dark Current Correction and Reflectance Calibration

Dark Current Correction is a preprocessing technique used to reduce the intrinsic noise of a sensor. In the context of MSI, where our sensors are multispectral cameras, the sensor noise is captured as an image. We can then subtract this noise from every image in our dataset. While this step does not fully remove intrinsic noise, it can greatly reduce it.

Reflectance Calibration is another preprocessing tool. By using a reference patch in the captured data (in our case, the inside of a Spectralon), we can then normalize the channels, a process that will affect the results of PCA and ICA.

In our reference paper, both steps were used in the same order as preprocessing steps. We added these steps to the preprocessing stage and examined how they affected the results.

In this experiment, our main goal is to test if dark current correction and reflectance calibration can actually improve the visibility and separation of the undertext from the overtext.

If we instead reduce the number of channels from 16 to 12 (still through PCA) and then apply ICA to extract 3 components, we obtain components for text, texture and noise. The texture component follows:

![ICA texture patch](/assets/img/posts/week8/pca_rgb.png)

![ICA texture patch with new steps](/assets/img/posts/week9/img_4_1846_recorte_normal.png)

While the new steps did not meaningly affect the RGB recomposition, the text component or the noise component, they did not degrade image readability. The texture component, especifically, showed more promising results: near the center of the image, some parts of the undertext are now visible. 

Although the undertext is still not easily readable, the results of this experiment suggest these new preprocessing steps can be crucial for the overall success of the pipeline.
