# Week 11 - Literature Revision

## Overview

I spent Week 11 reading articles related to similar works to our project. My main goal was researching more about the field and identifying other approaches to explore.

While some of the papers do not correlate directly to our topic, I'll discuss my main findings for each paper and how they relate to our theme. 

## Articles

### Readability Enhancement and Palimpsest Decipherment of Historical Manuscripts

The first article I read, ["Fabian Hollaus et al."](https://www.academia.edu/82529906/Readability_Enhancement_and_Palimpsest_Decipherment_of_Historical_Manuscripts), discusses the use of PCA, ICA and LDA in the context of palimpsest recovery.

While we have not yet tried LDA in our pipeline, the paper makes an argument for its use. Through experimentation, the authors argue LDA might out perform both ICA and PCA, making it an interesting possible addition to our pipeline.

LDA is a supervised machine learning algorithm that works by using labeled samples to maximize the distance between different classes. Therefore, despite its reported better results, we must first find a way to label at least some of our pixels before applying LDA, which is not required in either PCA or ICA.

### Analytical and mathematical methods for revealing hidden details in ancient manuscripts and paintings: A review

["Anna Tonazzini et al."](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2090123219300037)

This second article is an overview of analytical methods for revealing hidden details in ancient texts, with a somewhat heavy focus on palimpsests and MSI. The paper covers many different pipelines and classical algorithms such as PCA and ICA. A particular pipeline that interested me was the use of only some of the channels of the RGB reconstruction from the MSI cube, specifically R and B, to reveal the palimpsest. This choice was done due to the undertext being particularly more red than the overtext in the analysed paper.

Another direction is the use of Spectral Unmixing algorithms and SOMs for blind source separation. We'll also discuss these in future posts.

### Understanding multispectral imaging of cultural heritage: Determining best practice in MSI analysis of historical artefacts

["Cerys Jones et al."](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1296207419308064)

The paper discusses and recommends best practices for the use of MSI in analysis of historical artefacts, focusing on standard notation and practices to allow for better repeatability. Furthermore, the paper also provides a major overview of the field, grouping sources based on methodology.

My main takeaways from this article are first the recurring use of LDA in the field and the use of two new techniques: Spectral Angle Mapper (SAM) and Spectral Mixture Analysis (SMA).

SAM is technique used to measure the difference between two spectral signals. Therefore, if we conclude the spectral differece between undertext and overtext is too low, then we there is a major reason to suspect the texts cannot be separated with classical methods, which relates to our next paper.

### Revealing Palimpsests with Latent Diffusion Models: A Generative Approach to Image Inpainting and Handwriting Reconstruction

["Mahdi Jampour"](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2025W/ImageQuality/papers/Jampour_Revealing_Palimpsests_with_Latent_Diffusion_Models_A_Generative_Approach_to_WACVW_2025_paper.pdf)

This paper discusses the use of Generative AI to image inpainting and palimpsest recovery. The goal is to use GenAI to reconstruct parts of an image that are completely covered, even with little to no information on what the hidden part looks like.

While the use of GenAI also comes with issues like hallucinations and trustworthiness of results, the paper introduces some ways to encourage the model into using surrounding information to hallucinate less and general more accurate results.

Additionally, the paper also discusses the use of a pre-existing dataset to train the new model.

We'll discuss possible uses of GenAI and this dataset in later weeks.
