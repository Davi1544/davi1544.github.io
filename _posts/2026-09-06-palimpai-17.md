# Week 17 - A New Direction

## Overview

As mentioned in Week 16, I am now shifting my focus towards generative AI. This is also connected to the fact that I am taking a Deep Learning course this semester, for which I need to develop a project. I decided to use this opportunity to explore whether a generative model could help with the PalimpAI problem.

The main idea is to combine the multispectral information we have with a Latent Diffusion Model. Instead of trying to recover the undertext directly from the spectral data, the model will be trained to reconstruct the parts of the manuscript hidden by the strike-through.

One of the main difficulties is that we do not have real ground-truth images of the hidden text. To address this, I will create a synthetic dataset using real spectral information from our corpus. The idea is to take text that is already visible and replace its spectral response with samples of graphite and graphite mixed with ink, creating examples where the original text is known but partially covered.

The generative model will then receive a three-channel representation of the multispectral data. I will compare different ways of reducing the original 16 spectral channels to three channels, including PCA, MNF, and a small convolutional autoencoder. These representations will be used to fine-tune a pretrained Stable Diffusion Inpainting model using LoRA.

The goal is not to replace the classical methods we have explored so far. I will continue keeping them in mind and using them where appropriate, but for the next part of the project I will focus more heavily on the generative approach and see what it can actually achieve.

Since generative models can produce plausible but incorrect content, any reconstruction will be treated as a hypothesis rather than definitive evidence of what the original text contained.

## Next Steps

The next step is to build the synthetic dataset and start setting up the diffusion model and training pipeline.
