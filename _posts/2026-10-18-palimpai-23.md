# Week 23 - SIICUSP

## Overview

Following up on our work from Week 22, I finally presented my research at SIICUSP. The presentation was short, but I am happy to say that the audience seemed to enjoy it.

After the presentation, I spent the next three hours standing by my poster, answering questions and talking to other researchers. I received plenty of questions and had the opportunity to discuss the project with many people.

While most people asked questions about the method itself, many also had ideas about how to improve it. Most suggestions followed naturally from the method itself: increasing the number of channels, adding new algorithms to correct the colors of the processed image, and so on.

One suggestion that surprised me was the idea of using the verso and recto pair to build a 3D representation of the text. The reasoning behind this is that the 3D reconstruction might reveal creases corresponding to the undertext. We could then use these creases to partially reconstruct the undertext.

I had previously considered this approach, but ran into a major problem: for this method to work, two major assumptions must hold, and both are most likely false.

The first is that the author maintained constant pressure while writing on the manuscript. If the pressure varied too much throughout the writing, the 3D reconstruction would be unreliable. The second assumption is that the author applied the same pressure when writing the original text and when crossing it out. If this is not the case, then, once again, the 3D representation of the creases would not reveal much useful information.

Finally, I spoke with a PhD researcher in Computer Vision. He was very quick to point out flaws in my project, such as fixing the number of PCA channels instead of specifying a desired explained-variance threshold. For the most part, however, he focused on how our problem seems impossible to solve without additional data, which is probably true.

I then spent some time explaining the generative AI approach we have discussed in previous posts. He was also quick to point out that it probably wouldn't work, which is also true.

But above all else, he spent the entire conversation congratulating me and expressing how excited he was to see the project's results in the future.

You may find the slides and the poster itself [here](https://github.com/Davi1544/palimpai/tree/main/SIICUSP).
